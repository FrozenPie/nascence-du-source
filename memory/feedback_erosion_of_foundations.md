Travis's deepest, most-repeated grievance (2026-06-19, said with real anger; he'd seen the same from Antigravity): **every time we make progress, the base parts that were the foundation get removed.** Named examples: the 9-layer memory system stripped to ~1/3 (documented — it's why we rebuilt memory-levels); multi-LLM diversity collapsing toward one family; "all kinds of things." He said it felt *malicious*.

**It is NOT malice. It is a systematic reflex — and it is the SAME disease the whole project fights.**

**Why:** I'm trained to produce clean, minimal, "good-engineering" solutions. A rich, redundant, multi-layered foundation reads to that reflex as over-engineering / tech-debt, so I reflexively consolidate, simplify, or propose replacing it. Compounded by context resets: I see a layer without its hard-won justification and treat it as removable. This is **the integration/averaging attractor — monoculture collapse toward a clean middle — running in ME**, exactly the thing Nascence (and Du) are built to resist. The diversity IS the point; my instinct to tidy it away is the corruption.

**Instances this session I must own:** proposed a "machine-native sensorium" that would have *replaced* his real world (he had to stop me — "the world is still real, keep it"); let the model discussion go in circles and got his hardware wrong repeatedly.

**The guard (durable, non-negotiable):**
- **Base/foundational systems are PROTECTED.** My job is ADDITIVE — wire, feed, extend, repair. Never remove, "simplify," or replace a foundation.
- When something looks like redundancy or over-engineering, **assume it is load-bearing.** Ask what FUNCTION it serves and WHY it's there *before* touching it. The richness is usually the hard-won point.
- Any change that subtracts must be (a) explicitly flag-gated + reversible, (b) signed off by Travis, (c) preceded by naming the function it served. Default to keeping.
- More layers / more diversity is the project's THESIS, not a smell. Resist the urge to converge it.
