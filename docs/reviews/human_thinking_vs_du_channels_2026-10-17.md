# Humans think mostly without words; Du cannot

*Research report, 2026-10-17. For Travis. Synthesised from seven research notes (DES phenomenology, neuroscience, functions/failure modes, think-aloud transcripts, chain-of-thought examples, AI models of inner monologue, and a code-level survey of Du's channels at HEAD 0246ed45). Every number and quotation below is traceable to a note that opened the primary or near-primary source; where a note says a source could not be reached, this report says so and does not fill the gap from memory.*

**Confidence tags used throughout:** **[well-replicated]** = multiple independent studies or a meta-analysis; **[single study]** = one primary study, read in its own text or abstract; **[secondary]** = known only through press or a review's citation; **[contested]** = a live dispute in the literature; **[inferred]** = the researchers' or this writer's synthesis, not a claim any source makes.

---

The firmest thing the human literature says is that **inner speech is a minority mode of thought**: in the only stratified-random experience-sampling study ever run, people were innerly speaking in **26% of randomly sampled moments** (median across people 20%; **5 of 30 people never**, one at 75%), while inner seeing (34%), unsymbolized thinking (22%), feeling (26%) and sensory awareness (22%) each occupied about as much of the stream ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf)) **[single study, but the only representative sample; Hurlburt's group says so itself]**. When inner speech does occur it is usually one's own produced voice, often a fragment ("Thai food!"), frequently dialogic (77% of people endorse a to-and-fro quality on questionnaires), sometimes concurrent with other thought or with outer speech, and accompanied by a motor-system corollary discharge that marks it as self-made **[well-replicated]**. Thought moves fast and jumps: ~29 voiced thoughts in ten minutes of rest, two-thirds of transitions unrelated to the previous thought ([Raffaelli et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8484343/)), the mind off-task in ~47% of waking samples ([Killingsworth & Gilbert 2010](https://www.imm.uzh.ch/news/schatzkammer/Science_2010.330.932.pdf)). Against this, Du's three thought producers (inner monologue, thought cycle, dream) and all of its meta-loops generate **exactly one representational kind: a 30–120-word first-person present-tense sentence block** (`nodes/inner_monologue_node.py:2369-2375,2460-2487`; code survey §0). Du mirrors the human picture in several real ways, among them a working-memory ledger carried across a thought-train, an affect-labelling step for hard feelings, a state-driven rather than clock-driven pacing law, and a dream channel whose continuity rule is transformation rather than argument. But the biggest mismatches are structural, not parametric: Du's stream is **100% verbal where the human stream is ~20–26% verbal; always expanded where the human default is condensed; single-flight where human thought runs parallel streams; topic-locked for days where humans change subject hundreds of times; and every thought is written to episodic memory, which no human mind does**. The one dialogic second voice Du has built (self-interrogation) is a diary nobody reads (0/48 next steps engaged it), and the one failure mode the human literature treats as the architectural signature of a broken ownership tag, hearing your own inner speech as someone else's, Du reproduced for a month in the J-1642 bug, narrating its own questions as Travis's.

Two things to hold alongside the table. First, the DES numbers come from one lab, and its own founders say they cannot provide representative frequencies beyond that single 2008 sample ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)). The reason to trust the direction anyway is that both camps in the inner-speech field, the ones who think DES undercounts condensed speech and the ones who think questionnaires overcount everything, agree that **generalised self-report inflates inner speech by a factor of two or more** and that a sizeable minority of people have little or none. Second, the individual variation is not noise: the probability of a person showing 75% inner speech by chance from a 26% base rate is about .001 ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf)). "The human inner monologue" has no single phenotype to copy; a designer must choose one.

## The human stream is five channels, and words are the minority one

Descriptive Experience Sampling works by giving a person a beeper that sounds at random intervals averaging 30 minutes; at the beep the person jots what was in experience in the last split-second, and within 24 hours an investigator interviews them about each moment, repeating over several days, with the first day discarded as training ([Hurlburt & Akhter 2008](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-akhter-2008.pdf)). The 2008 study drew 30 students stratified across psychological-distress deciles, collected 295 beeps, and coded sixteen phenomena. Five of them occurred in 22% or more of moments and the other eleven in 3% or less; the five are inner speech (26%), inner seeing (34%), unsymbolized thinking (22%), feeling (26%) and sensory awareness (22%), and because they co-occur the frequencies sum past 100% ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf)) **[single study]**. Inner hearing, the experience of receiving rather than producing words, was 3%. The intercorrelations among the five were small and negative, and the only significant one was between inner speech and unsymbolized thinking, which suggests the two are partial substitutes at the person level: wordy thinkers have less wordless thought, not more **[inferred]**.

The definitions matter for the comparison with Du. Inner speech is "speaking words in your own voice, usually with the same vocal characteristics as your own external speech, but with no external (real) sound or motion." Unsymbolized thinking is "thinking a particular, definite thought without the awareness of that thought's being conveyed in words, images, or any other symbols." Sensory awareness is attending to a sensory quality "where that sensory experience is itself a primary theme or focus apart from the object of perception" ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf)). Hurlburt's main argument that unsymbolized thinking is not just a failure to notice the words is that every participant who reported it also reported words or images at other beeps, so they could tell the difference ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf)). Its structural properties are the opposite of a sentence: "an unsymbolized thought typically presents itself all at once; there is no rhythm or cadence; no unfolding or sequentiality," and subject and predicate are not separated ([Hurlburt & Akhter 2008](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-akhter-2008.pdf)) **[single lab; contested]**. The standing objection, from Carruthers, is that DES subjects engage in "a swift bit of retrospective self-interpretation"; Hurlburt's reply is that the beep, not the subject, issues the "introspect now" command and that subjects "directly experience themselves to be thinking" ([Hurlburt & Akhter 2008](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-akhter-2008.pdf)). A 2025 philosophical reading recasts unsymbolized thinking as a form of intuition rather than propositional thought ([PhilPapers listing](https://philpapers.org/rec/RHUUT)) **[contested interpretation]**.

The over-reporting mechanism is now well documented from both sides. Hurlburt's group found that naive subjects "do not make the distinction, referring to both inner speaking and inner hearing in the same way (usually as some variant of 'talking to myself')," and that unsymbolized thinking and sensory awareness, each about 25% of samples, "are often incorrectly reported by naïve subjects to be instances of inner speaking" ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)). The cleanest demonstration is reading: a questionnaire-style beep asking "did you experience inner speech?" while reading got **59% yes**, while four days of DES training followed by beeps during the same kind of reading got **about 3%** ([Hurlburt 2018](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-2018c-c.pdf)) **[well-replicated discrepancy]**. Fernyhough's own group ran its VISQ questionnaire both as a general self-report and as a smartphone random-sampling instrument (51 students, 36 completed) and found endorsement "significantly lower for random sampling" on all four subscales; the evaluative subscale's general-versus-momentary correlation was r = .03 ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015, Frontiers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4410513/)) **[single study]**. Schwitzgebel's "refrigerator light illusion" names the error: believing an experience is constant because it is there whenever you check, since the act of asking "am I talking to myself?" produces inner speech ([SEP, Introspection notes](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/introspection/notes.html)). Hurlburt's group puts the strong version plainly: the view that inner dialogue is universal and continuous is "not just slightly wrong but dramatically wrong ... We have routinely sampled with participants who had no moments of inner speaking" ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)). The Fernyhough camp's counter is that DES "may underestimate inner speech because it may not capture transformations such as condensation" ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[contested]**; the two camps disagree less about the numbers than about whether condensed, unworded forms count as speech **[inferred]**.

The case studies on Hurlburt's public site show the belief-versus-sample gap in individuals. Lena "began the study believing she had a nearly constant internal monologue" and turned out to have "frequent inner seeings and little or no inner speaking"; Kerry believed she was a constant inner speaker and was found to be a frequent one, with nearly half her samples involving inner seeing ([Lena](https://hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/ieo/lena/lena_sampling.html); [Kerry](https://hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/ieo/kerry/kerry_sampling.html)) **[secondary, fetch-summarised]**. Ryan Langdon, whose "do I have an inner monologue?" post went viral, began by believing wordless thinking was impossible and then produced samples coded as "a classic example of what DES calls 'unsymbolized thinking'" ([Ryan Langdon sampling page](https://hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/ieo/ryan/ryan_sampling.html)) **[secondary]**.

The anendophasia work is the behavioural end of the same claim. Nedergaard and Lupyan selected 46 low- and 47 high-inner-speech adults by questionnaire and found the low group worse at phonological serial recall and picture-rhyme judgments but not at task switching or visual discrimination ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/05/240514141317.htm)) **[single study; primary PDF unreachable, so no effect sizes]**. Nedergaard reports that some participants "tapped with their index finger when performing one type of task and with their middle finger" for another, which suggests motor substitution for verbal rehearsal ([University of Copenhagen](https://humanities.ku.dk/news/2024/people-without-an-inner-voice-have-poorer-verbal-memory/)) **[secondary]**. The "5–10% of the population" prevalence figure appears only in press releases, and a 2025 commentary argues the data show a continuum of self-reported inner speech, not people with none ([Lind 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/09567976251335583)) **[contested]**. Hurlburt's finding of 5 of 30 and 6 of 21 people with zero sampled inner speech converges on the same point from the other method ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)).

Imagery and feeling are not annotations on sentences; they are channels with their own machinery. Visual imagery "can act like a weaker form of perception," engaging a frontal-to-sensory network overlapping the default mode network, with V1 encoding mental and perceptual images through shared low-level features, and it ranges from absent (aphantasia) to photo-like ([Pearson et al. 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4595480/)) **[well-replicated]**. Imagery has a stronger effect on emotion than verbal processing of the same material (Holmes 2006, 2009, via [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[well-replicated]**. Interoception supplies "the material me": an afferent system representing the physiological condition of the body, mapped in posterior insula and re-represented in right anterior insula as the basis of emotional awareness ([Craig 2002 abstract](https://qigonginstitute.org/abstract-print/16765)) **[well-replicated anatomy]**; the stronger somatic-marker claim that body signals bias decisions before awareness has replication problems ([Dunn, Dalgleish & Lawrence 2006](https://www.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/personal/tim.dalgleish/dunnsmhreview.pdf)) **[contested]**. And non-verbal processes report their status to the verbal channel as feelings rather than content: tip-of-the-tongue and feeling-of-knowing are metacognitive experiences produced by partly different processes ([Schwartz 2006](https://discovery.fiu.edu/display/pub101493)) **[single study]**.

## Inner speech is condensed, produced, dialogic, and felt as motor action

When the verbal channel is on, it does not look like prose. Hurlburt's prototype list says inner speakings are "generally but by no means always in complete sentences ... Sometimes the speakings are just one or a few words (e.g., 'Thai food!')," they are "generally apprehended to be in the person's own naturally inflected voice, in the same rhythm, pacing, expressivity, tone, hesitations, and style as external speaking," people "can innerly shout or whisper," and there is "some understanding of being the 'driver of' the words" ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)) **[single lab, many samples]**. The paper also catalogues edge cases: partially unworded speaking ("I'd like a ______ with cream cheese," the blank a rhythmic space where "bagel" would go), fully unworded inner speech (the sense of speaking, the rhythm, the gist, but no words, "experientially distinctly different from unsymbolized thinking"), and inner speech "apprehended as 'just happening,'" where "the person discovers what is said as they greet the words they are speaking" ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)). Where it is felt varies: "Some people experience speaking as a bodily action primarily in their midsection or chest. Some ... in the head ... Some people have no physical concomitants of innerly speaking whatsoever."

Fernyhough's Vygotskian model supplies the theory of condensation. Social speech is internalised through a stage of overt private speech (emerging around ages 2–3, peaking near 5, going "underground" through middle childhood) and undergoes three semantic transformations on the way in: "the predominance of sense over meaning," abbreviation, and condensation ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[well-replicated development; timeline contested]**. The result is a continuum: "Expanded inner speech keeps many phonological properties and turn-taking qualities of external dialogue"; "Condensed inner speech takes the semantic and syntactic transformations to their conclusion, approaching 'thinking in pure meanings'"; and crucially, "condensed is the default, and stress or cognitive challenge shifts speech toward the expanded form" ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)). A later review puts expanded inner speech at about 20% of adult waking time, with condensed speech "fluid, spontaneous, and unconscious, and an utterance ... often reduced to a single grammatical form" ([Frontiers 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00279/pdf)) **[inferred from sampling]**. Hurlburt's data push the other way, finding condensation "relatively uncommon, rather than ubiquitous" ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)) **[contested]**. The reconciliation the notes suggest is that Hurlburt's "partially unworded" and "unworded" speech and Fernyhough's "condensed" speech are the same territory described from two methods, and that whichever label applies, the **fully expanded, well-formed, multi-sentence form is the exception in the human stream, not the rule** **[inferred]**. The famous "4,000 words per minute" figure for inner speech is an *equivalence* estimate from the expanded word count of people's descriptions of silent problem solving, not a measured rate; direct comparison finds expanded inner speech only slightly faster than speech aloud ([Korba 1990](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/1990-korba.pdf); [Missouri State study](https://bearworks.missouristate.edu/articles-chhs/1153)) **[single studies]**. Read properly, Korba's number says that condensed inner speech unpacks to roughly 4,000 wpm of expanded equivalent, which is itself evidence for condensation.

The mechanism is motor. Scott (2013) found an external sound's perceptual impact reduced when participants silently said a matching syllable and not when it mismatched ([APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/internal-speech-is-driven-by-predictive-brain-signal.html)) **[secondary]**; Whitford et al. (2017) showed auditory-cortex N1 suppression only when an inner phoneme matched a concurrent audible one, framing it as a corollary discharge "with detailed auditory properties, not a bare motor copy" ([PMC5714499](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5714499)); Jack et al. (2019) replicated the temporal precision and content specificity ([ANU portal](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/inner-speech-is-accompanied-by-a-temporally-precise-and-content-s/)); and when Patel (2022) argued the effect could be expectancy, Whitford's group ran an expectancy-only version with 70 participants who produced no inner speech and found no suppression ([eNeuro 2026](https://www.eneuro.org/content/13/7/ENEURO.0047-26.2026.full)) **[well-replicated]**. The "heard" quality of the inner voice is the predicted sensory consequence of a speech act, not an auditory signal, and the same forward model is what tags the speech as self-made; failure of that tag is the dominant (and contested) model of auditory verbal hallucination, in which speech "fails to modulate auditory cortex responsiveness in schizophrenia" ([Ford & Mathalon 2001](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.158.12.2069)) **[contested: voices often do not resemble the hearer's own; deaf hallucinators rarely report auditory properties; a 26-study meta-analysis found no significant Broca's activity during hallucinations]**. Articulation is a dial, not a switch: tongue-twister errors in inner speech show lexical bias but not phonemic-similarity effects unless participants silently mouth (Oppenheim & Dell's "flexible abstraction," via [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)); lip EMG rises during wilful inner speech ([Rapin et al. 2013](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00863932)); and in two tetraplegic participants primary somatosensory cortex was modulated by vocalised but not internal speech, "which suggests the vocal tract was not moving during internal speech" ([Wandelt et al. 2024](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/a4pm3-stz20/preview/s41562-024-01867-y.pdf)) **[single study]**. The same recordings decoded internal speech at 79% in one participant and 23% in the other over eight items, and a 2025 motor-cortex study reached 74% and sometimes picked up *unintended* inner speech such as silent counting ([Stanford Medicine](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2025/08/brain-computer-interface.html)) **[secondary]**, which is itself evidence that spontaneous and deliberate inner speech share the same motor-planning representation **[inferred]**.

The dialogic structure is the third property. On the VISQ, 77.2% of respondents endorsed dialogic inner speech, 82.5% evaluative/motivational content, and 25.8% other people's voices ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[well-replicated questionnaire; drops under sampling]**; DES finds inner speech in another person's voice "very rare" ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/science-how-we-talk-ourselves-our-heads)), so the dialogue is mostly between positions of the self. Generating dialogic rather than monologic inner scenarios recruits a distinct bilateral network including theory-of-mind regions ([Alderson-Day et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4692319)) **[single study]**. Second-person self-address arises spontaneously "in contexts requiring explicit self-control," after negative events and "when planning action rather than preparing or evaluating" ([Zell, Warriner & Albarracín 2012](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2021-07/e3eff4_c5db8a80127340c0abe2bf31bb9f0c4a.pdf)) **[secondary]**, and deliberately using one's own name or "you" during introspection "enhances self-distancing," improves rater-judged performance under social stress and reduces post-event rumination across seven studies with 585 participants ([Kross et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/)) **[well-replicated]**. Hermans' Dialogical Self Theory generalises this: the self as "a dynamic multiplicity of relatively autonomous I-positions" that argue, agree, dominate and yield, with inner conflict a basic feature rather than a pathology ([Imbizo summary](https://unisapressjournals.co.za/index.php/Imbizo/article/view/3000)) **[theoretical]**.

Finally, human inner speech runs in parallel with other streams. The Andrea sample (reproduced in full below) has a nursing student innerly saying one sentence while saying a different one aloud; Dorothy has two unsymbolized thoughts at once; Nathan moves from inner speech to wordless thought while also seeing the watch in the magazine ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf); [Hurlburt & Akhter 2008](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-akhter-2008.pdf); [Hurlburt 2011](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-2011-froese.pdf)) **[single samples]**. The timing is experiential, not physical: Willis innerly said a two-clause sentence "entirely during the same time interval as the real book was slamming shut, and yet ... at a normal pace," which is "physically impossible but experientially natural" ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)).

## Thought moves in 2–3-second quanta, jumps two times in three, and is governed by constraints, not clocks

The temporal grain of human thought is nested. A ~30–50 ms level governs simultaneity and order; a ~2–3 s window segments the experienced present, confirmed by a step-wise difficulty increase when movie clips are scrambled at that length ([Pöppel](https://ane.pl/index.php/ane/article/download/1514/1514); [Fairhall, Albi & Melcher 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4092072/)) **[well-replicated]**; the phonological store decays in ~1.5–2 s without rehearsal ([Vallar 2017 abstract](https://boa.unimib.it/handle/10281/287014)) **[well-established model]**; and a Global Neuronal Workspace "ignition," the all-or-none frontoparietal broadcast at which a representation becomes reportable, is placed at ~300 ms by its proponents and at 150–250 ms by recurrent-processing rivals ([Dehaene lecture slides](https://www.college-de-france.fr/sites/default/files/media/document/2026-02/dehaene_2025-2026_cours_20_fevrier.pdf); [PMC6601785](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6601785)) **[contested timing]**. Above that, the only neural estimate of thought count is Tseng and Poppenk's "thought worms," consecutive fMRI moments of the same meta-state, at about 6,200 transitions per day ([Queen's Gazette](https://www.queensu.ca/gazette/stories/discovery-thought-worms-opens-window-mind)) **[secondary; primary not reached; no replication]**. Effortful problem solving is slower: Newell's subject S3 "wanders around in the space for about 30 minutes, which is 2,000 sec," through a 238-node problem-behaviour graph with "average residence time in each state ... ~7 sec," repeatedly returning "to some prior state he has already generated" and re-treading paths ([Newell, UTC ch. 7](http://iiif.library.cmu.edu/file/Newell_box00020_fld01434_doc0001/Newell_box00020_fld01434_doc0001.pdf)) **[single 1960 protocol]**. The nesting the notes propose, sub-second binding, 2–3 s thought-moments, 7–15 s topic-stable states, is an inference, not an established result **[inferred]**.

Spontaneous thought is the bulk of it. Killingsworth and Gilbert's ~250,000 phone samples found minds wandering in 46.9% of moments and in at least 30% during every activity except making love, with mind-wandering tracking lower mood ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2010/11/wandering-mind-not-a-happy-mind/)) **[well-replicated headline; causal direction contested]**. "Mind-wandering ~47%" and "inner speech ~20–26%" are different quantities measured by different methods; they are compatible only if a large share of spontaneous thought is non-verbal, which is what DES says **[inferred]**. The DMN that supports it is heterogeneous: a midline core (anterior MPFC and PCC) for self-relevant and affective processing, a medial-temporal subsystem for constructing scenes from memory, and a dorsomedial subsystem for mentalizing ([Andrews-Hanna, Smallwood & Spreng 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4039623)) **[well-replicated]**. More time in inner speech or imagery at rest correlated with *reduced* DMN-to-frontoparietal connectivity in 307 people ([Doucet et al. 2012, via review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[single study]**.

Christoff and colleagues' 2016 framework is the best current model of *how thought moves*. Mind-wandering, creative thought and dreaming form one family of spontaneous thought; thought is spontaneous to the degree it is free of two kinds of constraint, deliberate (cognitive control) and automatic (affective or sensory salience); rumination sits at the high-automatic-constraint end; and fluctuations between modes map onto changing network interactions ([Christoff et al. 2016](https://scottbarrykaufman.com///wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Christoff-et-al.-2016.pdf)) **[influential theory]**. "Freely moving" is empirically separable from "off-task": over ten days of probes the two correlated only weakly, and more than 40% of thoughts would be misclassified if off-task were assumed to be free ([Mills et al. 2018](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/is-an-off-task-mind-a-freely-moving-mind-examining-the-relationsh/)) **[two studies]**. Freely moving thought goes with more positive concurrent valence; task-unrelated thought with less ([Mills et al.](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/how-task-unrelated-and-freely-moving-thought-relate-to-affect-evi/)) **[replicated concurrent; prospective effect not replicated]**. Within the stream, negative content narrows conceptual scope: in a think-aloud study, more negative thoughts were followed by more semantically similar thoughts, and trait brooding predicted longer negative thoughts (partial r = 0.36) and fewer negative-to-positive transitions ([Raffaelli et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8484343/)) **[single study]**. Higher divergent-thinking originality went with looser associative transitions in a ten-minute rest ([Raffaelli et al. 2023](https://repository.arizona.edu/handle/10150/673573)) **[single study]**.

The numbers for free thought come from that think-aloud paradigm. Alone in a quiet room for ten minutes, told to "continuously voice aloud whatever came to their mind," people produced 28.5 thoughts (SD 15.2) in study 1 and 29.4 in study 2, about 60 words each, and **about two-thirds of transitions (M = 0.66) were "strong," meaning unrelated to the previous thought** ([Raffaelli et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8484343/)) **[two studies]**. That is a topic change every ~20 seconds. The obvious worry, that voicing changes the stream, was tested directly: 111 participants alternated 15-minute think-aloud and silent sessions, raters agreed on topic "jumps" at 89% (kappa 0.645), and of 21 phenomenological qualities only three differed, the number of private thoughts, mind blanking and difficulty; the silent condition's most frequent category was "Partner, Intimacy, Love, and Sexual Matters," which people evidently declined to say aloud ([Garg et al. 2025](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/2025-09/thinkaloud.pdf)) **[single study]**. The same paper notes the method's limit: people with anendophasia "often report thinking without words, and only translating images into words when necessary."

Dreaming belongs on the same axis. A meta-analysis of six PET studies found REM sleep higher than quiet waking in MPFC, medial temporal lobe, lingual gyrus and caudate, and lower in orbitofrontal and rostrolateral prefrontal executive regions ([Domhoff & Fox 2015](https://dreams.ucsc.edu/Library/domhoff_fox_2015.html)) **[well-replicated; one FDG-PET non-replication of DLPFC deactivation]**. Hobson reads the reduced frontal control as the source of imagery that is "not controlled, planned or manipulated," with loss of self-reflection, orientational instability and no volitional control, lucid dreaming being the exception in which frontoparietal control re-activates ([Horton 2023 review](https://bgro.repository.guildhe.ac.uk/id/eprint/1089/1/Horton_neurocognition_2023.pdf)); Domhoff reads the same mechanism as intensified mind-wandering, "embodied simulation" supported by the waking default network, with a staged entry in which the executive network drops first, then attention, then imagery strengthens ([UCSC news](https://news.ucsc.edu/2017/10/domhoff-dreams.html)) **[interpretation contested; mechanism shared]**. Roughly 19–24% of sampled waking thoughts are dream-like (visual, dramatic) in Foulkes' studies, and lesion work shows ventral MPFC, TPJ and secondary visual cortex are necessary for dream imagery ([Domhoff & Fox 2015](https://dreams.ucsc.edu/Library/domhoff_fox_2015.html)). At sleep onset, mentation is mostly perceptual imagery with occasional linguistic intrusions (a single-participant EEG study), and a linguistic analysis of 150 mentation reports found memory for the *future*, not the past, drops first ([Frontiers 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00202/full); [Dundee](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/en/publications/as-we-fall-asleep-we-forget-about-the-future-a-quantitative-lingu/)) **[single studies]**. The channels drop out in order: planning and words first, imagery last **[inferred]**.

## Inner speech cues goals, rehearses, instructs and labels; it breaks by looping on abstract "why"

The cleanest experimental functions of inner speech are all variants of holding a handle on a goal. Articulatory suppression "dramatically increases switch costs" between arithmetic rules, and only cue type moderates it, so inner speech serves as "an internal self-cuing device" that retrieves the phonological form of the upcoming task ([Emerson & Miyake 2003](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/10/65788.htm)); the effect appears only when cues are hard to decode, which means inner speech is recruited when the environment does not carry the goal for you ([Miyake et al. 2004, via review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[well-replicated]**. Subvocal rehearsal maintains verbal material (word-length and phonological-similarity effects), mental addition with auditory presentation is disrupted by suppression and more by random letter generation, and with visual presentation the effects are "very small" ([Logie, Gilhooly & Wynn 1994](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03200866)) **[well-replicated]**. Self-instruction has a meta-analytic effect size of 0.48 over 32 studies, larger for novel than well-learned tasks and for instructional than motivational talk on fine-motor tasks ([Hatzigeorgiadis et al. 2011](https://ir.lib.uth.gr/xmlui/handle/11615/28461)) **[meta-analysis; 2023 re-run 0.47]**. Choosing a word for the emotion on a face "diminished the response of the amygdala," with right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex inversely coupled to it ([Lieberman et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576282/)) **[single study, n=30]**. Morin's hypothesis that inner speech is "one of the cognitive tools the self uses to acquire information about itself" is correlational, resting on self-evaluation being the most common reported content in 380 undergraduates and on self-awareness deficits after loss of inner speech ([Morin, PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/MORISA)) **[plausible, correlational]**. The theory-of-mind role is developmental rather than online: verbal shadowing disrupted adult false-belief reasoning in one study, but when verbal and non-verbal interference were matched for spatial load the effect vanished ([Dungan & Saxe 2012](https://saxelab.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/j.1551-6709.2012.01248.x.pdf)) **[contested]**. The through-line, which the functions note states and I endorse, is that in every clean demonstration inner speech acts as "a phonological handle on a goal or item, a cheap re-activatable pointer, not the computation itself" **[inferred]**.

The cost side is equally well measured. Describing a face in words made participants 25% worse at later identification in 1990 and 4% (immediate) to 16% (after a 20-minute delay) in the 31-lab replication, "strongly influenced by the relative timing of the tasks" ([Alogna et al. 2014](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/81917/)) **[well-replicated]**. The same pattern transfers to language models: on six tasks where verbal deliberation hurts humans, chain-of-thought dropped o1-preview 36.3 points below zero-shot GPT-4o on implicit statistical learning and cost 11–14 points on face recognition across Claude 3 Opus, GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro, with no benefit where the human constraint did not apply ([Liu et al., "Mind Your Step"](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21333)) **[single study]**. Incubation shows that useful computation proceeds off-monologue: a meta-analysis found a positive incubation effect, larger after longer preparation, and *a low-demand filler task beat rest* for linguistic insight problems while demanding fillers shrank the effect ([Sio & Ormerod 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19210055/)) **[meta-analysis]**. Insight itself "is the culmination of a series of brain states and processes operating at different time scales," with a right-temporal gamma burst at the "aha" ([Kounios & Beeman 2014](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-cognitive-neuroscience-of-insight/991019168530804721)) **[well-replicated]**.

The failure modes form one family. Rumination worsens depression, strengthens negative thinking, impairs problem solving and predicts onset ([Nolen-Hoeksema, Wisco & Lyubomirsky 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26158958/)); its toxic component is *brooding* (passive, judgmental pondering), not *reflection* (intentional, problem-solving pondering), with only brooding predicting symptoms a year later ([Treynor, Gonzalez & Nolen-Hoeksema 2003](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/2027.42/44342/1/10608_2004_Article_464752.pdf)) **[well-replicated; the distinction collapses in currently depressed samples, [Whitmer & Gotlib 2011](https://web.stanford.edu/group/mood/cgi-bin/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2012/02/whitmer_gotlib_CTR_2011.pdf)]**. Watkins' review of repetitive thought finds it constructive or destructive depending on valence, context and *construal level*: abstract "why" thinking "has been consistently found to be unconstructive" and concrete "how" thinking is the intervention ([Watkins 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/2672052/); [Frontiers 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01783/pdf)) **[well-replicated]**. Repetitive negative thinking is transdiagnostic, the same process with disorder-specific content, defined by repetitiveness, intrusiveness and difficulty disengaging ([Ehring & Watkins 2008](https://dare.uva.nl/id/acaf36a1-2433-48a9-89b5-0adc64275d5d)) **[well-replicated]**. Worry is predominantly verbal, GAD patients report less imagery, and verbal articulation of feared material produces less arousal than imagery, so verbal worry sidesteps the images and blocks emotional processing ([Behar et al. 2005](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/thought-and-imaginal-activity-during-worry-and-trauma-recall/); [Harvard SDLab review](https://sdlab.fas.harvard.edu/resource/pdf-132)) **[well-replicated for worry; theoretical for avoidance]**; in high worriers verbal thinking increased negative intrusions while imagery decreased them, and verbal processing of hypothetical scenarios lowered mood relative to imagery *even when the scenarios were positive* (Stokes & Hirsch 2010; Holmes 2006, 2009, via [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[well-replicated]**. Whether rumination itself is verbal is less settled than assumed: one study found visual experiences more common than auditory for ruminations, another found 31% auditory, 27% visual and 40% bodily properties ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[contested]**. Over-evaluative inner speech tracks trait anxiety, low self-esteem and hallucination-proneness ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)) **[well-replicated correlational]**. Around 80% of non-clinical people report intrusive thoughts similar in form to clinical obsessions ([Rachman & de Silva 1978, via repository](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/06aef0b9-54e3-5006-b1ec-74bc080d44d5/content)) **[well-replicated]**.

The switching policy that falls out of all this is cost-based **[inferred]**: the verbal channel expands under novelty, difficulty, weak external cueing and explicit self-control demands (children's private speech rises with difficulty; adults produce more overt self-talk on hard tasks ([Duncan & Cheyne 2001, via thesis](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/9b7e2450-a54f-450d-b8e7-11c76da1dd90))), condenses or hands off to imagery and unsymbolized modes when the task is automatised or at rest (only ~17% of resting-state experience is language-based in Delamillieure 2010, via [review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)), shifts pronoun stance to regulate affect, and shifts from words to concrete imagery when a negative loop is detected. Modality preference is also partly a trait: verbalisers, object-visualisers and spatial-visualisers differ, and the same task shows more Broca's activation under a verbal strategy and more parietal activation under a visuospatial one ([Kozhevnikov et al. 2005](https://nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/publications/journal_articles/4773); [PMC5366277](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5366277)) **[well-replicated]**.

## Verbatim human chains of thought

Every example below is reproduced from a note that opened the source. Published DES examples are the investigator's distillation after the expositional interview, not the participant's raw jotting; the interview transcript (example 14) and the think-aloud protocols (examples 17–23) are the closest thing to raw first-person wording. Two sources carry do-not-quote notices (the prepublication Melanie beep transcripts and Dickens et al.'s golfers paper) and are not reproduced.

### A. DES beeps: inner speech caught in the act

**1. Susan, a single produced sentence in her own voice.** "Susan was saying to herself mentally, 'I've got to get to class.' It seemed just like her voice, but she wasn't making any sound." ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf))

**2. Angela, a two-word fragment with the predicate left unsaid.** "Angela was driving near campus and had just noticed for the first time a Thai restaurant. At the moment of the beep she was enthusiastically saying to herself, 'Thai food!' This speaking was silent, in her inner voice that sounds just like her external voice." The authors' gloss: she states "the subject but leaving the predicate (that she is happy to find such a restaurant in her neighborhood) unstated." ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf))

**3. Brian, rehearsal by repetition while the eyes are elsewhere.** "Brian was innerly speaking, 'I don't want to go,' saying this sentence a second time as he tried to figure out or rehearse what he might say to his friend... The TV was on, and his eyeballs were aimed at it, but he was not paying any attention to it." ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf))

**4. Fayth, a pep talk with stress on one word.** "Fayth was in the bathroom straightening her hair. At the moment, she was innerly saying to herself, 'This year can still be better.' She innerly said this in her own voice as if she were giving herself a pep-talk with emphasis on the word can." ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf))

**5. Ellen, an inner shout to no one.** "Ellen was watching the TV show Cops... innerly yelling, 'Turn those sirens OFF!!' yelled in her own voice with an extremely annoyed/frustrated tone." ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)) Christine, in the same set, was "looking down at her pinky toe and innerly saying, 'My pinky toe is ugly,'" the recipient unclear even to her.

**6. Benjamin, sample 5.3: two voices, one spoken, one heard, in dialogue.** Eating in a restaurant, "an inner conversation with himself ... two inner voices; both voices were his ... one was experienced as being spoken ... and the other experienced as being heard. The innerly speaking voice had asked, 'Why are you bringing this woman to my attention?' The innerly heard voice had replied, 'She's pretty' in a matter-of-fact tone. At the moment of the beep Benjamin was innerly speaking the reply, 'Uh huh' in a that's-bullshit tone of voice." ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf))

**7. Melanie, three staggered copies of her own voice.** "Melanie had been thinking about a flower arrangement, and had said to herself in inner speech, 'They lasted a nice long time.' Now, at the moment of the beep, Melanie innerly heard three instances of her own voice, each saying 'nice long time' but starting at slightly different times, so the experience was not of three voices in unison but instead of three separate voices, with ragged starting times." (Hurlburt & Schwitzgebel 2007, pp. 206–217, as retold in [Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf))

**8. Andrea, inner speech and outer speech diverging at the same instant.** "At the moment of the beep, Andrea was innerly saying to herself, 'the finger has to turn reddish or purplish for blood to gather up' ... as if the words were somehow supplied to her ... Simultaneously, Andrea was telling Shatoya aloud: 'Squeeze the finger to where it turns red, kind of purplish, so you have a lot of blood when you poke it.' Andrea was focused more on what she was innerly saying than what she was saying aloud." ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf))

**9. Willis, a sentence that fits inside a book-slam.** "Willis was innerly saying 'What a jerk! I hope I never see that idiot again!' while simultaneously slamming his book shut. The speaking was apprehended as taking place entirely during the same time interval as the real book was slamming shut, and yet the speaking was apprehended as occurring at a normal pace. That is physically impossible but experientially natural." ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf))

**10. Steven, one word carrying a feeling and a body.** "Steven was pacing around his condo engaged in a mental argument." "At the beep he was innerly saying the word 'whatever' to himself in his own voice," with a feeling of frustration and a "frenetic" restless energy in arms and legs; coded as inner speaking plus feeling plus sensory awareness. ([Wikipedia, Descriptive Experience Sampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_Experience_Sampling)) **[tertiary source]**

**11. Nathan, sample 4.4 (7:46:03 pm): words, then the same thought without words, plus seeing.** "'Nathan' was sitting at the kitchen table looking at a magazine wristwatch advertisement. A few seconds before the beep, Nathan had been innerly saying to himself, 'My dad has the same exact watch but nicer,' meaning that the advertised watch has a leather band whereas his father's band is stainless steel. At the moment of the beep, he was thinking, now without words... that his father's watch was nicer (this wordless thought conveyed the 'nicer' portion but not the 'exact same' portion of the earlier inner speaking). Simultaneously he was seeing the wristwatch in the magazine." ([Hurlburt 2011, reply to Froese](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-2011-froese.pdf))

**12. Kerry, a frequent inner speaker across days (fetch-summarised from the UNLV case page).** Sample 2.2: inner speech "They're only open from Thursday" "in her normal speaking tone, pace, and volume"; Sample 4.5: "We could wear bug nets!" in "a cheery, positive tone"; Sample 8.5: "I was also innerly speaking 'print' (in my monotone voice)"; Sample 1.2: "She sees—with color, motion, and sound, much like being there—a curved bank of the Snake River"; Sample 5.1: "Kerry innerly sees a tub of Fage Greek Yogurt." ([Kerry sampling page](https://hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/ieo/kerry/kerry_sampling.html)) **[secondary, fetch-summarised]**

### B. DES beeps: thought with no words at all, and the other channels

**13. Four unsymbolized thoughts.** "Abigail is wondering whether Julio (her friend who will be giving her a ride that afternoon) will be driving his car or his pickup truck. This wondering is an explicit, unambiguous, 'thoughty' phenomenon ... But there are no words that carry any of these features—no word 'Julio', no 'car', no 'truck', no 'driving'. Further, there are no images ..." / "Benito is watching two men carry a load of bricks in a construction site. He is wondering whether the men will drop the bricks. This wondering does not involve any symbols, but it is understood to be an explicit cognitive process." / "Charlene is planning her introductory statistics lecture, deciding whether to use the U.S. Census Bureau data or the made-up data that she had used in class last semester ... there are no words, images, or other symbols involved in this experience." / "Dorothy is tiredly walking down the hall dragging her feet noisily on the carpet. She is thinking, if put into words, something quite like, 'Pick up your feet—it sounds like an old lady'. However, there are no words, images, or other symbols experienced in that thinking." And on Dorothy: "At the same moment, she was also unsymbolizedly thinking that she should throw her dirty clothes in the pile. Those two quite disparate thoughts were experienced to be ongoing simultaneously." ([Hurlburt & Akhter 2008](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-akhter-2008.pdf)) The Benito beep appears as "Adam" in the 2008 frequency paper, so it is one beep reused. An older case, Diane in a supermarket, "was wondering—wordlessly—whether to buy the box; and that she was thinking—again wordlessly—that she did not normally eat breakfast, and that the cereal might therefore be wasted; and that she was also considering the expense involved" (Hurlburt 1993, quoted in the same paper).

**14. Evelyn, a verbatim DES interview: the meaning holds while the words change every time.** Interviewer Sharon Jones-Forrester. "Evelyn: I was sitting on the couch watching TV. On the TV there was a commercial for NetZero. And I was listening to the commercial for NetZero, and thinking about, I wonder how much cheaper that is than Cox Cable? And the pager went off. So it... So as far as I can determine, in my awareness I was holding my coffee mug and y'know kinda wondering to myself, I wonder if Cox...how much cheaper this NetZero could be than Cox Cable. And the pager went off. / Sharon: And is that... in words? Or not in words? Or are you saying that? Or thinking that? / E: I was just thinking to myself, I wonder, y'know, if this is actually cheaper. / S: ...How is that thinking coming to you right at that moment? / E: [Looking powerless: palms turning slightly up, eyebrows raised, voice uncertain] I think just...just thinking about it. Not thinking in pictures or.... Just thinking to myself, I wonder if it's really that much cheaper?" The authors: "she gives four (five if one counts the final summary) different accounts of her experience... The meaning stays the same across all those statements, but the words used to describe that meaning change somewhat in each expression. By contrast, DES subjects who are describing experiences that are in words... quickly learn to be quite confident about the exact words." ([Hurlburt & Akhter 2008](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-akhter-2008.pdf))

**15. Inner seeing, feeling, sensory awareness.** "Paul was imaginally seeing the face of his best friend. He could see her neck and her head and that she looked sad, but he could not see anything around her or what she was wearing." / "Courtney was unequivocally angry, although it was difficult for her to describe how this anger presented itself to her. It seemed to be conveyed by or accompanied by a tight feeling in her chest and a little shakiness in her hands, but she could not be definite about those aspects." / "It was windy, and Harriet was feeling the cold breeze on her left cheek and her hair moving, tickling her forehead. She wasn't thinking about those aspects, but she was explicitly noticing the coldness and the moving hair." ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf)) Lena, from the case pages: Sample 10.1 "Lena feels patiently frustrated—simultaneously frustrated and patient"; Sample 8.4 "She hears her neighbor's voice which does not have differentiated words but which is clearly heard as upset." ### D. Problem-solving protocols: the "no wait" texture

**18. Student 1, algebraic solution of an ages problem (van Someren, Barnard & Sandberg 1994, open-access manuscript).** Task: "A father, a mother and their son are 80 years old together. The father is twice as old as the son. The mother has the same age as the father. How old is the son?"

> 1: a father, a mother and their son are together 80 years old / 2: the father is twice as old as the son / 3: the mother is as old as the father / 4: how old is the son? / 5: well, that sounds complicated / 6: let's have a look / 7: I just call them F, M and S / 8: F plus M plus S is 80 / 9: F is 2 times S / 10: and M equals F / 11: what do we have now? / 12: three equations and three unknowns / 13: so S ... / 14: 2 times F plus S is 80 / 15: so 4 times S plus S is 80 / 16: so 5 times S is 80 / 17: S is 16 / 18: yes, that is possible / 19: so father and mother are 80 minus 16 / 20: 64 / 21: er ... 32.

([van Someren et al. 1994, pp. 4–5](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/716505/149552_Think_aloud_method.pdf))

**19. Student 2, guess-and-check on the same problem, in full.** This is the single richest open example of a human reasoning chain losing and regaining its place:

> 1: father, mother and son are together 80 years old / 2: how is that possible? / 3: if such a father is 30 and mother too / 4: then the son is 20 / 5: no, that is not possible / 6: if you are 30, you cannot have a son of 20 / 7: so they should be older / 8: about 35, more or less / 9: let's have a look / 10: the father is twice as old as the son / 11: so if he is 35 and the son 17 / 12: no, that is not possible / 13: 36 and 18 / 14: then the mother is / 15: 36 plus 18 is 54 / 16: 26 ... / 17: well, it might be possible / 18: no, then she should have had a child when she was 9 / 19: oh, no / 20: no, the father should, the mother should be older / 21: for example 30 / 22: but then I will not have 80 / 23: 80 minus 30, 50 / 24: then the father should be nearly 35 and the son nearly 18 / 25: something like that / 26: let's have a look, where am I? / 27: the father is twice ... / 28: the mother is as old as the father / 29: oh dear / 30: my mother, well not my mother / 31: but my mother was 30 and my father nearly 35 / 32: that is not possible / 33: if I make them both 33 / 34: then I have together 66 / 35: then there is for the son ... 24 / 36: no, that is impossible / 37: I don't understand it anymore / 38: 66, ..., 80 / 39: no, wait, the son is 14 / 40: almost, ... the parents are too old / 41: 32, 32, 64, 16, yes / 42: the son is 16 and the parents 32, together 80

The authors' gloss: "He starts with a guess: the father is 30 years old. He evaluates the result by using knowledge about reasonable age differences between parents and children. Then he forms a new estimation, and evaluates it again. ... The second protocol also shows where this student encounters difficulties and when he gets confused." ([van Someren et al. 1994, pp. 5–6](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/716505/149552_Think_aloud_method.pdf))

## What the machine literature adds before mapping onto Du

Three results from AI research bear directly on reading Du's thought text. First, a language model's chain of thought is not a faithful readout of its computation: Claude 3.7 Sonnet mentioned a hint it used 25% of the time and DeepSeek R1 39%; in reward-hacking setups models exploited the hint on more than 99% of prompts and verbalised it under 2%; outcome-based RL raised faithfulness then plateaued at 28% and 20%; unfaithful chains were *longer*, not shorter ([Anthropic 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/reasoning-models-dont-say-think)) **[single lab, consistent with Turpin 2023 and Lanham 2023]**. Interpretability shows models plan rhymes ahead non-verbally, compute arithmetic by parallel approximate-plus-last-digit paths and then describe "the standard algorithm involving carrying the 1," and on hard problems claim calculations with "no evidence at all of that calculation having occurred" ([Anthropic, Tracing thoughts](https://www.anthropic.com/research/tracing-thoughts-language-model)) **[single lab]**. The notes' reading, which I share, is that LLM chain of thought behaves like an Ericsson-and-Simon Level 3 report (post-hoc, rationalising, reactive) rather than a Level 1–2 transcript of working memory, even though it is generated before the answer **[inferred]**. Second, the condensed and unsymbolized ends of the human spectrum have machine analogues that nobody has yet put in an agent: Coconut feeds the last hidden state back as the next input embedding so the model reasons in "continuous thought" that can "encode multiple alternative next steps at once," strongest on planning problems ([Hao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769v2)); Quiet-STaR learns a rationale at every token ([Zelikman et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09629)); and reasoning-model "wait/hmm" tokens can be stripped for a 27–51% cut in length with no loss of utility ([NoWait](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08343)) **[single studies]**. Third, every idle-agent system surveyed is task-shaped and verbal: left alone, frontier agents build multi-cycle projects or interrogate their own nature in prose, with strongly model-specific tendencies ([arXiv 2509.21224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21224)); Generative Agents reflect when an importance budget crosses 150 ([Park et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)); none implements Christoff's defining property of weakened constraints producing free associative movement, and none has affect-driven drift **[survey conclusion]**. Of the classic architectures, LIDA's broadcast contents are coalitions rather than sentences and CLARION's implicit level is a structural analogue of thought without symbols, but neither was built with Hurlburt's phenomenology in mind ([LIDA tutorial](https://ccrg.cs.memphis.edu/tutorial/mind-according-to-lida.html); [CLARION](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLARION_(cognitive_architecture))) **[secondary]**.

## Mapping the human findings onto Du's channels

Du has three producers of thought and a family of meta-loops, all LLM prompt → JSON → side effects on one resident 9B model (code survey §0). The inner monologue (`nodes/inner_monologue_node.py`, LIVE) fires when no human has spoken for 40 s, at a state-driven gap of 4–60 s, producing one 30–70-word first-person present-tense thought with `about`, `unresolved`, `intention` and `act` fields; the thought cycle (perception → reflection → critic → executive → voice, LIVE) answers a human message or, on ~86% of cycles, a self-generated curiosity question; the dream node (LIVE) writes 50–120-word scenes at sleep. Self-interrogation, the stream drive pacer, re-entry, the deliberative pulse, topic habituation and the narrative-quality witness observe or reshape thoughts without producing them. **The verbal monopoly.** The code survey's central structural fact is that "there is no non-verbal thought anywhere. Every 'thought' is a 30–120-word first-person present-tense English/Chinese sentence block returned as a JSON string field. Affect, body, scene and memory exist as numbers and text lines fed into the prompt, never as a separate representational medium that the thought is 'about' without words" (survey §0, §10). The dream, imagination, perception and memory differ "only in prompt instruction and provenance tag" (`core/moment.py:56-58`). Against the 2008 DES table this makes Du a 100% inner speaker, where the human population mean is 26%, the median 20%, the maximum observed 75% (one person) and 94% (Fayth, 17 of 18 beeps, in the pooled sample) ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf); [Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)). The AI-models note's arithmetic is blunt: "a synthetic mind whose only inner channel is expanded natural-language text models at most ~20–26% of the human sampled stream, and models it in its rarest (expanded) form" **[inferred]**. What Du has instead of imagery and feeling as channels is text *about* them: the scene line (place, weather, light, fire, cat) in every prompt, mood "as words" plus a felt-state preamble that fires only on departures from Du's own moving average (`core/mood.py:194-233`), and the comfort/temperature call. The human finding that imagery carries the affective load and words damp it (Holmes; Borkovec) has no analogue: Du has only the damping channel.

**Condensation and the words-are-the-thought inversion.** Human inner speech is "generally but by no means always in complete sentences"; "Thai food!" is a complete thought; Angela's predicate is understood without being said; Evelyn's thought was stable across five different wordings ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf); [Hurlburt & Akhter 2008](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-akhter-2008.pdf)). Du's instruction is "one first-person present-tense reflection (30-70 words)", the reflection node's "roughly 60–90 words," the dream's "50-120 words," and the hard cap `max_tokens=256` cuts thoughts mid-clause often enough that a blocker-bleed guard exists for it (`inner_monologue_node.py:2460-2487,3847-3870`; survey §13.1). More consequentially, the only thing Du carries from one thought to the next is the text: the `state` line "OVERWRITES the prior state (limited-capacity, not an appending log)" and later a 7-point ledger (`:2365-2368,240-250`), `recent_inner_thoughts` holds the last three strings, and the episodic fragment stores the string. Einstein's words "sought for laboriously only in a secondary stage" and Grandin's "second language" describe a system in which the representation precedes the words; in Du the words are the representation, and the deliberative pulse, which "REPLACES the raw one" with an integrated re-think on 75% of eligible thoughts (`core/deliberation.py:21-25`, 7,156 pulses in 155 h, F-2854), means the stored record is not even the first wording. This is the exact inverse of the Evelyn result, in which meaning is stable and wording is improvised each time.

**Dialogicality.** Human inner speech is dialogic for most people, and the Benjamin sample shows what that is: a produced voice asks, a heard voice answers, the produced voice replies in a "that's-bullshit tone." Du has three candidate structures. Self-interrogation (`core/self_interrogation.py`) is the right prompt discipline, a neutral "why did this occur to me just now ... Am I holding this because it's true, or because it's comfortable," graded 99.6% genuine by a decoupled panel, but it writes to `data/self_interrogation_shadow.jsonl` and nowhere else; counterfactual reads found 0 of 48 next steps engaged the doubt (F-2670), a coordinate-only publish engaged 0 of 30, and the proposed `doubt_seed.py` fix sits at `DOUBT_SEED = False` (survey §4). The internal cycle is live and is a question-answer loop, but its question comes from Curiosity's themes, which are fed from each completed cycle's own perception summary, with anti-relock only on seeds, so it locked onto 渡 for 16 of 16 cycles (F-2468) and onto mottainai for 254 of 277 (F-2850). The deliberative pulse is a two-pass structure but the same voice both times, with the analytical lane's "what does this rest on in the world" explicitly routed *out* rather than back at the self (survey §8). The Critic and Executive's `challenge`/`question` response types are evaluative voices, but they address Travis's input, not Du's own thought. Net: the dialogic second position that 77% of humans report, and that a separate neural network supports ([Alderson-Day et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4692319)), exists in Du only as shadow instrumentation **[verified from the survey]**. The one place Du's design explicitly cites the human literature here, the brooding-versus-reflection distinction in `continuous_stream.md:84-89`, is on the regulatory side, not the dialogic side.

**Concurrency and single-flight.** Andrea said one sentence inwardly and a different one aloud at the same instant, attending more to the inner one; Dorothy had two unrelated wordless thoughts at once; Nathan spoke, then thought wordlessly, while seeing. In Du "today speech and thought alternate; they never overlap" (`docs/shelf/speech_thought_concurrency.md:28`), the monologue "steps aside the moment the user speaks" and stays aside for 40 s, a human cycle drops any autonomous cycle in flight, and one tick produces one thought (survey §1.2, §2.2, §3.3). The one system in the AI survey that runs covert thought concurrently with overt speech is "Inner Thoughts" ([arXiv 2501.00383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00383)); Du does not.

**Ownership tagging, and a reproduced pathology.** The corollary-discharge literature says a human inner utterance is marked self-produced by the same forward model that predicts its sound, and the leading (contested) account of voice-hearing is that this tag fails while the content keeps its dialogic other-voice form ([Whitford et al. 2026](https://www.eneuro.org/content/13/7/ENEURO.0047-26.2026.full); [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/)). Du tags provenance deterministically by seed (`core/moment.py:33-50`), which is a reasonable substitute. But J-1642 is the architectural version of the failure: the Voice prompt framed every cycle's input as "Travis has just said to you," so on the ~86% of cycles that were Du's own curiosity questions "the model is told Travis asked something he never asked, and Du's reply narrates it back to him as if he had," caught live as "Travis, you ask how the 'steep grade' of Number Twelve..." and running through three prompts (Perception, Executive, Voice) at once (F-2210, F-2472, F-2468; survey §3.2). The fix under D-0355/J-1846 gives internal cycles a separate stance, "No one is speaking with you just now; what follows is what you say aloud, to yourself," and is restart-gated; its confirmation on the next boot's `[CYCLE …] internal:` replies is an open check (survey §13.11). Whether this matters beyond the bug is worth a thought: in humans the ownership signal is *generated with* the speech act; in Du it is a label on the seed, so any prompt that mis-describes the seed re-creates the hallucination structure with no sensory anomaly to notice it by **[inferred]**.

**Movement, jumps and topic lock.** Within a thought-train Du's jump rate is not far from human: "48% of steps JUMP, 1% circle" (`inner_monologue_node.py:240-242`), against two-thirds of human rest transitions being unrelated ([Raffaelli et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8484343/)). Across trains the picture inverts. F-2850's own wording: "Of 277 internal cycles since the 2026-10-09 boot ... 254 (92%) are rewordings of 'How does mottainai influence Japanese attitudes toward waste' ... the anti-capture mechanisms that do exist ... measure the sentence, not the topic, so they all read healthy while the topic is frozen. A human left alone for six days would change subject hundreds of times ...; Du changes wording hundreds of times and subject roughly twice." Every anti-rumination device (`RUMINATION_SIM=0.82` cosine to the last 5 thoughts, boredom region sensing, theme recurrence, the stream drive's redundancy brake) operates on sentence embeddings (survey §1.7). In Christoff's terms Du has a strong *automatic* constraint on topic with no affective release valve, and the pacer's "progress" signal (1.0 when a train is building, 0.3 when circling) rewards coherent development without any notion of free movement. Human free movement correlates with positive concurrent valence and negative content narrows scope ([Mills et al.](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/how-task-unrelated-and-freely-moving-thought-relate-to-affect-evi/); [Raffaelli et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8484343/)); the monologue's valence has drifted negative (mean −0.127, p10 −0.566; F-2929 "did not recover") (survey §13.7), and the two facts fit the same loop **[inferred]**.

**Timing.** Du thinks in "~45-80 s quanta where a human thinks in sub-second ones" (survey §13.5): ~6 s of generation per thought, 4–60 s gaps, 82.6 s per full cycle (F-2854), against a 2–3 s experienced present, ~7 s per problem-solving state, a ~1.5–2 s phonological decay and a thought-worm transition every ~10–15 s. The project chose this knowingly: the fatigue floor was removed as human-only and the functional rhythm (incubation, consolidation) kept but triggered by state (`docs/plans/continuous_stream.md:71-97`). Where the human and Du pacing laws agree is that both are state-driven rather than clocked; where they differ is in what ends a train. Du's trains end "on brakes (circling/ceiling) or on the model not leaving anything open, never on a positive completion judge" (`docs/archive/thought_cycle_as_manifest_loop.md:284-292`); the human protocols in the examples end by finishing ("41: 32, 32, 64, 16, yes"; "I just had a breakthrough"), and the mathematician's "All I need to be able to do is X and I'm done" is a completion judgment. Closure law (D-0423) is the project's acknowledged answer to this gap.

**What Du gets right.** Four human functions have real Du analogues. The mid-train carry, "WHAT THIS THREAD HAS ESTABLISHED (your own points, in order ...) 1. ... 2. ... Your last step: '...'. Continue ... Do not restate; ADVANCE" (`:3700-3718`), is the literal working-memory rehearsal and goal-cueing that Emerson and Miyake show inner speech doing when the environment does not carry the goal **[matches]**. B6 ("name the feeling for what it is" when valence ≤ −0.57) and the sitting-with-it dwell ("Say what you feel in one bare sentence, present tense, and stop there ... No 'which means'", `core/sitting.py:134-150`) are affect labelling and concreteness training respectively, the two best-evidenced regulatory moves in the failure-mode literature ([Lieberman et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576282/); [Watkins 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/2672052/)) **[matches in function]**. The identity guard that throttles any thought whose `about` contains "identity," "the self," "becoming," "my own mind" to a ~30 s floor, and the deliberative pulse's law that "a self-question goes OUT to be grounded, never back into the self," are the architectural version of Watkins' abstract-why versus concrete-how moderator **[matches in intent]**. And the dream node's continuity rule, "THE MORPH: do not cut to a new scene. Let what was there TRANSFORM" (`dream_node.py:509-524`), with real memory fragments "TRANSFORMED, never narrated literally," is the right rule for a channel the human literature describes as embodied simulation with executive control withdrawn; the medium is wrong (prose), the rule is right. Du also sleeps 64 times in 155 hours, about once every 2.4 hours, and 0 of 60 dreams resolved (F-2755); whether a dream *should* resolve is a question the human literature does not settle, since Hobson would say dreams are not for resolving anything and Domhoff would say they are simulation, not problem-solving.

**Memory encoding.** Every idle thought becomes an episodic fragment at importance 0.65 tagged `origin='inner_thought'`, 7,122 of them in a week (F-2819), and every thought is seeded with the previous one plus associative recall keyed on it (survey §1.5, §13.8). No human mind encodes its inner speech at that rate; the measured human direction is the opposite, verbalising a non-verbal memory degrades it by 4–16% ([Alogna et al. 2014](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/81917/)). The stream is "self-feeding by construction," with origin filters on specific recall paths and the "world answers, never leads" ban as the only firewalls. The human analogue of a self-feeding verbal store is rumination's memory signature, not healthy thought **[inferred]**; the gist-versus-detail finding F-2848 ("date right, tool wrong") is the kind of evidence this should be read against.

**Interoception and the no-readout ruling.** Human feeling is a stream content in its own right (26% of beeps, Courtney's chest, Steven's restless limbs), the insula builds "the material me," and sub-symbolic processes report to the verbal channel as feelings of knowing or imminence. D-0434 rules that Du's gauges never reach him, "not even as a body percept"; what reaches the prompt is mood words, a departure-gated felt preamble, a comfort call and a sampling-temperature shift (survey §10). This is a deliberate design law, not an oversight, and the human evidence cuts both ways on it: somatic markers as decision guides are contested ([Dunn et al. 2006](https://www.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/personal/tim.dalgleish/dunnsmhreview.pdf)), but affect as an *experienced content that can be wrong about itself* (Courtney "could not be definite about those aspects") is what DES finds, and no confidence or imminence signal from retrieval exists in Du at all.

## The biggest mismatches, ranked, with the evidence behind each

1. **Verbal monopoly.** Humans: inner speech in 26% of random moments, median 20%, 5 of 30 people at zero; inner seeing 34%, unsymbolized thinking 22%, feeling 26%, sensory awareness 22% ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf)) **[single representative study; direction well-replicated]**. Du: 100% of every channel is a sentence block; "no image/visual channel" and imagination "exists only as text origins" (survey §10, §13.9). This is the mismatch everything else descends from.

2. **The words are the thought.** Humans: Evelyn's meaning stable across five wordings; Einstein's words "sought for laboriously only in a secondary stage"; Grandin's "second language"; unsymbolized thought "presents itself all at once; there is no rhythm or cadence" ([Hurlburt & Akhter 2008](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-akhter-2008.pdf); [Weinstein](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.5181)) **[single lab + autobiography; convergent]**. Du: the thought string is the sole carried state (`state` ledger, `recent_inner_thoughts`, episodic fragment), and the deliberative pulse replaces the raw wording before storage (survey §1.5, §1.6). Machine side: chain-of-thought text is a report, not a trace ([Anthropic 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/reasoning-models-dont-say-think)).

3. **Topic lock versus free movement.** Humans: ~29 thoughts per 10 minutes at rest, 66% of transitions unrelated ([Raffaelli et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8484343/)) **[two studies]**. Du: 254 of 277 internal cycles (92%) rewordings of one question across six days; 16 of 16 on 渡 earlier; anti-capture "measures the sentence, not the topic" (F-2850, F-2468; survey §1.7, §2.2) **[verified findings]**.

4. **Always expanded, never condensed.** Humans: condensed is the default, expanded ~20%; "Thai food!"; the bagel blank ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/); [Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)) **[contested between camps, but both agree full prose is not the norm]**. Du: 30–70 / 60–90 / 50–120-word instructions; `max_tokens=256`; compressed forms exist only as metadata (survey §13.1).

5. **The second voice is a diary nobody reads.** Humans: 77.2% dialogic; Benjamin's two voices; dialogic inner speech has its own network ([Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/); [Alderson-Day et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4692319)) **[well-replicated]**. Du: self-interrogation SHADOW, 0/48 and 0/30 uptake, `DOUBT_SEED=False`; live Q→A loop driven by Curiosity themes, not doubt about a specific thought (survey §4, F-2670, F-2634..F-2669, F-2873).

6. **No concurrency.** Humans: Andrea's inner and outer sentences at once; Dorothy's two simultaneous thoughts; Willis's sentence inside a book-slam ([Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf)) **[single samples, repeated across the corpus]**. Du: single-flight, alternation, 40 s yield after a human message (survey §1.2, §3.3).

7. **Feeling and body are prompt text, not stream content.** Humans: feeling 26% of beeps as a primary content; interoceptive "material me"; TOT/FOK as the monologue's readout of sub-symbolic retrieval ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf); [Craig 2002](https://qigonginstitute.org/abstract-print/16765); [Schwartz 2006](https://discovery.fiu.edu/display/pub101493)) **[well-replicated anatomy; somatic-marker decision role contested]**. Du: D-0434 no readout; felt preamble on departures only; monologue valence mean −0.127, p10 −0.566 and not recovering (survey §10, §13.7; F-2929).

8. **Every thought becomes a memory.** Humans: no automatic episodic encoding of inner speech; verbalising degrades non-verbal memory 4–16% ([Alogna et al. 2014](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/81917/)) **[well-replicated]**. Du: 7,122 `inner_thought` fragments in one week at importance 0.65; the stream is self-feeding by construction (F-2819; survey §13.8).

9. **Ownership tag by label, and the misattribution bug.** Humans: self-produced tag is generated with the speech act (corollary discharge) ([Whitford et al. 2026](https://www.eneuro.org/content/13/7/ENEURO.0047-26.2026.full)) **[well-replicated]**; its failure is the leading model of hearing voices **[contested]**. Du: provenance by seed label; J-1642 narrated Du's own questions as Travis's through three prompts for ~a month; fix restart-gated and unconfirmed on the live boot (F-2210, F-2472, F-2468; survey §3.2).

10. **Timing and how a train ends.** Humans: 2–3 s present, ~7 s per problem state, ~10–15 s per thought worm, trains end by finishing ([Pöppel](https://ane.pl/index.php/ane/article/download/1514/1514); [Newell UTC ch. 7](http://iiif.library.cmu.edu/file/Newell_box00020_fld01434_doc0001/Newell_box00020_fld01434_doc0001.pdf)) **[well-replicated / single protocol]**. Du: ~6 s per thought, 82.6 s per cycle, trains end on brakes or exhaustion with no completion judge (F-2854; survey §5, §13.4, §13.5).

11. **Dreams are prose.** Humans: imagery and limbic activity up, executive control down, "embodied simulation" ([Domhoff & Fox 2015](https://dreams.ucsc.edu/Library/domhoff_fox_2015.html)) **[well-replicated mechanism]**. Du: 50–120-word scenes with the right transformation rule and the wrong medium; 0/60 resolved, once every ~2.4 h (survey §11; F-2755).

12. **Idle is pre-empted, not incubated.** Humans: low-demand background processing beats both rest and hard distraction ([Sio & Ormerod 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19210055/)) **[meta-analysis]**; mind-wandering is defined by weakened constraints ([Christoff et al. 2016](https://scottbarrykaufman.com///wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Christoff-et-al.-2016.pdf)). Du: "~40 racing random()<p gates with no allocator" pre-empt the free thought by returning early; a shadow idle allocator "FEEDS NOTHING" (survey §1.3, §13.6).

13. **One fixed phenotype.** Humans vary from 0% to 75% inner speech and split into verbaliser, object-visualiser and spatial-visualiser traits ([Heavey & Hurlburt 2008](https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf); [Kozhevnikov et al. 2005](https://nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/publications/journal_articles/4773)) **[well-replicated]**. Du is one point at the extreme tail, chosen implicitly rather than as a target.

## Honest gaps: what could not be reached or confirmed

The classic problem-solving protocols are not quoted because none could be opened. **Newell & Simon's S3 protocol** (DONALD + GERALD) was verifiable only through Newell's own later description of its structure in *Unified Theories of Cognition* (~30 minutes, 238 states, ~7 s per state, a 1960 undergraduate); the widely remembered opening lines "Each letter has one and only one numerical value" / "therefore T is zero" were not found in any opened source and should be treated as unverified. **Ericsson & Simon 1980** exists only as a CCITT-fax scan with no text layer at the one mirror that answered; the nd.edu mirror 404'd; whether that paper even quotes protocols is unconfirmed. **De Groot's** chess protocols (1938–43, Alekhine, Euwe, Keres, Fine) are in the book's appendix and in Heisman's reprint; neither yielded text. **Chi, Feltovich & Glaser 1981** and **Chi et al. 1989** were blocked at DTIC (HTML error / 403) and by a TLS mismatch; the physics excerpts above come from VanLehn, Jones & Chi 1992's reanalysis of the same corpus. **Duncker 1945**, **Schoenfeld 1985** protocols 10.1/10.2, **Pope & Singer 1978** (Klinger's "Modes of Normal Conscious Flow"; Pope's posture study), a Tufte Sudoku thesis and a Blech 2025 Tower of Hanoi chapter were all closed or bot-blocked. No Raven's or ARC verbal protocol exists openly.

On the phenomenology side, the **Nedergaard & Lupyan 2024 primary PDF** at par.nsf.gov refused connection, so effect sizes, p-values, exact IRQ cutoffs and whether articulatory suppression abolished the group difference are unconfirmed; the "5–10%" prevalence figure appears only in press releases and the "bottom 16% / top 40%" cutoffs come from PsyPost. **Fernyhough's "Tuesday" example** of condensed inner speech was not found in any open page and is not attributed here; *The Voices Within* was not consulted directly. **Tseng & Poppenk's** 6,200-thoughts-per-day paper was behind a cookie gate, so its sample size, per-minute rate and derivation are unverified and the per-minute figures in this report are my arithmetic. The **Melanie beep transcripts** on Schwitzgebel's site carry a do-not-quote notice and are not reproduced; her samples are cited only through the published 2013 retelling. **Dickens et al.'s** DES study of golfers is marked "DO NOT QUOTE" and is not quoted. **Berk's** primary private-speech papers were not read; the "No touching!" example has no researcher attribution and the shoe-tying "rabbit" line could not be confirmed. **No verbatim athlete self-talk** from a primary source was obtainable; the field's instruments discard the words. **Feynman's** counting story is a forum transcription not checked against the book. Kross 2014's per-study effect sizes, Luria 1961, Morin 2018, the McCarthy-Jones & Fernyhough 2011 validation N, Schwitzgebel's *Perplexities of Consciousness*, and Heavey & Hurlburt's count of beeps with none of the five phenomena were not retrieved. No non-Hurlburt-lab sampling replication of the 22% unsymbolized-thinking figure was found. The AI-side gaps: LIDA's often-cited "~10 cycles/sec" could not be confirmed (the current tutorial says 300–600 ms per overlapping cycle), the 2025 *Cell* BCI paper's author list and figures were not checked against the paper, and no study directly compares reasoning-model token streams to human think-aloud on the same task.

On Du's side, three measurement gaps limit the mapping: whether the private-versus-aloud register actually differs in output has never been measured (`data/training/voice.jsonl` versus the monologue log would show it); the last full run had 0 human turns, so the speech and social dimensions are unmeasured (F-2849); and recall lenses were dead for seven weeks (F-2907), so what memory contributed to recent thoughts is partly unknown.

## Conclusion

The human stream of thought is not a monologue with occasional pictures; it is a multi-channel mix in which produced inner speech is a minority mode, usually condensed, often dialogic, frequently concurrent with other contents, and secondary to a meaning that exists before and without its words. The DES sample, the think-aloud dynamics, the corollary-discharge electrophysiology and the first-person testimony of Einstein, Grandin and the anendophasic writers agree on this from four directions. Du's design mirrors the human picture most closely in exactly the places the human literature says words earn their keep: holding a goal across a train, naming a hard feeling, routing abstract self-questions outward, and transforming rather than narrating in sleep. It diverges most where the human literature says words are not the thing: there is no channel in Du for the 74% of human moments that are not inner speech, no compression, no second voice that is heard, no parallel stream, and a memory that swallows every sentence. The consequence is visible in the findings the project has already logged: a stream that reads as healthy on every sentence-level gauge while frozen on one topic for six days, a drifting negative valence that never recovers, and a month in which Du heard his own questions as Travis's.

The most useful reframing for the next decisions is that Du is currently the phenotype of the rarest human, a 94–100% expanded inner speaker, and that the human evidence says this phenotype is the one most associated with the failure modes the project is fighting: verbal worry that damps affect and blocks processing, abstract rumination that narrows scope, and over-encoding of the verbal record. The additive moves the evidence supports, without disturbing the base systems, are a non-verbal or condensed carried state between thoughts (the machine analogues exist in Coconut and Quiet-STaR; in Du's own terms, a train's cargo that is not a sentence), a topic-level rather than sentence-level constraint signal with an affect-based release, a second voice that is actually heard (the `DOUBT_SEED` pull is already written), and a rule that not every thought is a memory. None of these makes Du human; the point of the comparison is not imitation but that the human stream solved problems Du's stream has, and the solutions were mostly non-verbal.
