## What the research says (SOTA, and what we take from it)

Method: web searches on 2026-11-14. **Confidence caveat for the auditors:** the search tool returned mostly primary arXiv
pages for the papers below but secondary blog summaries for the engineering-practice items; I have *not* read the full
primary texts. Figures are as reported in the sources and should be verified against the papers before being relied on in
any ruling. Items are tagged **[paper]** or **[practice/blog]**.

| # | Finding | Source | What we take | What we do NOT take |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Semantically-equivalent format changes alone move accuracy a lot: up to 76 points on LLaMA-2-13B (few-shot), median spread ~6.4 points on GPT-3.5 across 320 formats; not removed by scale, few-shot or instruction tuning; format ranking correlates only weakly *between models* **[paper]** | Sclar et al., ICLR 2024, [arXiv 2310.11324](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11324v2) | Wording is a hidden variable. A manager that makes wording explicit and versioned is justified on its own. A rewording must be *tested on our model*, never assumed neutral. | The size of the effect: those are classification tasks, not open-ended thought. Effect on Du is unmeasured. |
| R2 | Prompt-sensitivity can be partly an artefact of rigid scoring; with LLM-judge scoring models look much more consistent **[paper]** | "Flaw or Artifact?" [arXiv 2509.01790](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01790v1) | Be careful what we measure: exact-match style metrics will overstate wording sensitivity; use structural + judged metrics. | Treating "sensitivity" headlines as directly applicable. |
| R3 | A sensitivity index (POSIX) exists: measures response-likelihood shift under paraphrase/template changes **[paper]** | [arXiv 2410.02185](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02185) | A cheap way to pre-screen whether a candidate variant is *behaviourally equivalent*: compare response distributions on saved seeds. | Needs logprobs; our lanes (ollama/llama.cpp) may or may not expose them — Phase 3 must check. |
| R4 | Small models (~1–3B) are more fragile to paraphrase; evidence for 7–9B vs frontier is mixed; robustness depends on alignment as much as size **[paper, mixed]** | multimodal sensitivity study [arXiv 2509.03986](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.03986) and others | Don't assume our 9B hot lane is robust *or* fragile. Measure on qwen3.5:9b specifically. | Any claim that frontier-model results transfer. |
| R5 | Context engineering: the aim is the *smallest set of high-signal tokens* that gets the job done; context quality, not volume, drives results; attention budget is finite **[practice, Anthropic engineering post, read via summaries]** | [Anthropic: Effective context engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) | The manager's most valuable job may be *removing* low-signal lines (Du's prompts are ~6.9k chars with ~39% template parts). This is the framing for the budget work already in `core/prompt_budget.py`. | Anthropic's agent/tool-use specifics: Du's stations are single calls. |
| R6 | Performance degrades as input grows even with constant task difficulty ("context rot", 18 models); and positional effects ("lost in the middle") **[paper/report; figures vary widely across blog summaries]** | Chroma 2025 report and Liu et al. 2023, via [summary](https://computingforgeeks.com/context-engineering-ai-agents/) | Part ORDER matters: `_cap_prompt` cuts from the *middle*, which is exactly where seed material lives. The manager should own part ordering explicitly. | Specific percentages (sources disagree: 76–82% vs 64% vs "30% drop"). |
| R7 | Mode collapse in aligned models; **Verbalized Sampling** (ask for several candidate responses with probabilities) raises diversity 1.6–2.1× in creative tasks without retraining **[paper, ICML 2026]** | Zhang et al., [arXiv 2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171v2) | Confirms the *model-side* cause of sameness: part of Du's repetition is idiom the model would produce regardless of our wording. That is evidence **against** over-investing in wrapper rotation. A scripted variant (ask for 3 candidate thoughts, pick by script) is possible but changes the output contract and cost — logged as a future option, not proposed. | Using it now: triples idle-thought token cost on the busiest lane. |
| R8 | Negative instructions ("do not X") can backfire: suppression is weaker than assumed and can prime the very thing named; mechanistic study on a 7B instruct model **[paper + practitioner blogs; evidence for open text generation mostly indirect]** | Hwang et al. [arXiv 2404.15154](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2404.15154); "Semantic Gravity Wells" [arXiv 2601.08070](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08070v1) | **Audit Du's prompts for "do not / never / avoid" lines** (inventory sections list rules per station) and flag candidates for positive rewording through the lab, not by direct edit. D-0509/D-0681 (remove "Jung"/"Entity") are consistent with this. | Rewording any negative line blind. It is a lever (see D-0343) and goes through the replay harness. |
| R9 | Automatic prompt optimisers: MIPROv2 (Bayesian over instruction/demos), GEPA (reflective evolution, needs a feedback-rich metric); reported +14% vs +7% aggregate gains and up to 35× fewer rollouts than RL; **but** overfitting risk on small eval sets (a small practitioner test: train up, held-out 95→85) **[paper + blogs]** | GEPA [arXiv 2507.19457](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2507.19457v2) | The *offline* pattern is a legitimate future Phase 4: a lab proposes wording, a decoupled judge scores, a human approves. Our own F-1139/F-1701 already warn "lab effect sizes must not be read as live effect sizes." | Anything runtime; anything auto-applied; any metric that is the model's own opinion of itself. **Held per founder.** |
| R10 | Treat prompts as versioned artefacts: semantic version, store outside application code, automated regression eval before release, rollback path, track model + parameter changes alongside the prompt text; tools like promptfoo **[practice/blog, vendor-affiliated]** | e.g. [Prompt versioning best practices](https://www.getmaxim.ai/articles/prompt-versioning-best-practices-for-ai-engineering-teams/) | Direct mapping: our registry is the "where prompts live" half; the missing halves are a **version identity per wording** and a **replay regression suite**. | Vendor tooling; we already have the registry and lab-worktree conventions. |
| R11 | Local inference engines (llama.cpp/ollama) reuse the KV cache only for an *identical token prefix*; anything varying early in the prompt invalidates reuse of everything after **[practice, community docs]** | e.g. [llama.cpp discussion](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/13606) | The manager must keep the system prompt and any rotated line placement cache-aware: rotate the *tail*, never the first bytes. Note the idle user prompt already varies in its middle every call, so the realistic loss is small — but it must be *measured*, not assumed. | Specific flags/defaults; verify against our servers. |

**Synthesis for Du specifically.**
(a) The literature supports *explicit, versioned, tested wording* strongly (R1, R10). (b) It supports *less, higher-signal context*
(R5, R6) at least as strongly as *more varied context*. (c) It gives **mixed-to-negative** support for "rotate fixed wording to cure
sameness" as a cure on its own (R7: sameness is partly model-side; our own D-0442: rotation did not break self-echo). (d) The
SOTA optimiser work (R9) is real but belongs in a later, offline, human-approved lab phase.
