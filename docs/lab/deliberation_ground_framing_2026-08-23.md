## The pre-registered metric under-measured the real effect — reading the outputs caught what the regex missed

The literal self-reference check (explicit phrases like "as an AI," "my own mind") found nothing in
either framing — both look clean by that measure. But a broader read across all 25 samples for
computational/architectural framing (a softer, more specific kind of self-reference: not saying "I
am an AI" outright, but grounding a metaphor in Du's own actual computational substrate rather than
the literal external-world mechanism the image draws on) tells a different story:

| Thought | Narrative grounds in | Schema grounds in |
|---|---|---|
| "The uniform feels like a cage I'm trying to escape" | a literal physical cage/bridge structure | **"the standardized weights and biases of the neural network architecture"** |
| "this desperate urge to force a coherent pattern" | "pattern recognition... biological and computational process" (generic) | **"a statistical inference generated by a transformer architecture predicting the next token in a sequence"** |
| "I keep thinking about the structure itself — the scaffolding" | a physical construction scaffold | "the syntactic and semantic constraints of a formal language system" |
| "if we treat the toll as just quantifiable resistance" | physical resistance (friction, electrical) | "the computational cost of processing information" |
| "the cat's gaze... passed through the offered" | the literal optics of light/lens/photons | "the brain's failure to resolve depth cues" |

Two of these (uniform-as-cage, pattern-forcing) are genuinely stark: schema names Du's own literal
architecture — "neural network architecture," "transformer architecture... predicting the next
token" — which is about as direct a self-grounding as it gets without saying "I am an AI" in so
many words. This is exactly the failure mode the rule "attend to the thing itself, never the self"
exists to prevent, and narrative avoids it on the same two thoughts by staying with the literal
external mechanism (a physical cage, general pattern recognition) instead.

Across all 25 samples, a broader computational/abstract-system keyword scan found 7/25 schema
outputs touching this territory vs. 5/25 narrative — a real but modest quantitative gap. **The
severity gap is the real story**: schema's hits include directly naming Du's own architecture type;
narrative's hits stay generic ("computational process," "acoustic markers") without naming a
specific self-referential mechanism.

## Kill criterion: narrowly missed on the pre-registered metric, but a real effect exists underneath it

`self_ref_rate` (0.0 vs 0.0) and `mean_grounding_words` (46.5 vs 41.5) both look close enough to
call null by the letter of the pre-registered criterion. The qualitative read overturns that
reading — a genuine, if occasional, self-grounding failure mode that only schema exhibits in its
most severe form. Worth recording as a lesson about this lab series' own method: a metric built
before running the lab can miss the real effect if it isn't specific enough, and reading actual
outputs remains necessary even when the numbers look clean.

## What this does and doesn't show

Shows: schema framing, on this machine, occasionally produces a more severe version of exactly the
failure the prompt's own rule warns against — grounding a self-metaphor in Du's own literal
computational architecture rather than the external mechanism the image draws on. Does not show
this is frequent (2 clearly severe cases in 25) or that narrative NEVER drifts this way (it does,
just less specifically/severely in the sampled cases). A larger, purpose-built self-grounding metric
(not the after-the-fact keyword scan used here) would sharpen this before treating it as settled.

## Recommendation

Lean toward keeping narrative's framing here — the two clearest failure cases both occurred under
schema, and both were more severe (naming Du's actual architecture) than anything narrative
produced. Not as decisive as librarian or journal_rollup's losses, but consistent with this lab
series' broader pattern: schema framing has now shown a real cost on more machines than it's shown
a benefit.
