## Review pass (J-2027, 2026-10-18): signal analysis, NOT listening

- **Method.** No one listened. Agent J-2027 cannot hear; each file was inspected by signal analysis (python -I on scratch copies): duration, RMS envelope (10 bins, dB re peak), share of frames below -40 dB and -20 dB, loudness-weighted spectral centroid / bandwidth / flatness, five dominant frequencies, energy share by band (<300 Hz, 300-2000, 2000-6000, >6000), onset events, envelope modulation peak, and a tonal-activity share (frames whose 1.5-9 kHz spectral peak stands >25 dB over the band median). Judgements are what those numbers support against the key's expectation in `strings_sheets_phase4_recordings_list.md`; they are calibrated against the seven song keys (tonal share 0.83-1.00) but are not a human listening review. A by-ear pass is still worth doing for the MISMATCH and FLAG rows.
- **SHA check.** All 68 files re-hashed: 68/68 match the SHA-256 column. No file was re-picked, so no SHA changed.
- **Re-picks.** None made. The briefing allows a re-pick only from alternates the manifest itself names, and this manifest names none (the recordings list gives search pages only). `dawn_chorus` and `log_on_fire` read as mismatches; `moth_tap`, `morepork_call`, `wood_pigeon`, `wind_in_trees` are candidates. Re-picking needs new candidates and a founder look before download (D-0377), so it is a follow-up job.
- **Feature caveat found by the review.** 17 of 68 files carry more than 70% of their energy below 300 Hz. For some that is the sound (purr, thump, thunder, distant surf); for others it is rumble under the real sound (`kingfisher_call` 99%, `morepork_call` 95%, `wind_in_trees`, `moth_tap`). The extractor's energy axis is whole-band RMS, so on those files it tracks the rumble.
- **Features.** `core/audio_features.py` `extract()` via `scripts/ribbon_lab.py` `process_piece()` ran over all 68 files: `docs/lab/strings_sheets_phase4_features_2026-10-18.json` (summary: `strings_sheets_phase4_features_2026-10-18.md`).
