## Background

`core/world_consolidation.py` files reading/study memory fragments under a `chapter_id` pointing at the gist ("chapter") each fragment is about, via `sweep()`/`file_book_notes()`. A historical bug in the tag-matching predicate (`_cites()`/`_cites_clause()`, fixed at commit `2d678ab`) checked only ONE word from a title's `_title_signature()` as a `content LIKE` test against a candidate gist's entire raw content, discarding the rest of the signature — so titles collided onto the wrong gist purely because one of their words was short/generic (or, per J-1033/F-1419's own later refinement, sometimes even a multi-word title still collided the same way, since only one word was ever checked regardless of title length). A handful of gists absorb a disproportionate share of these wrong fits ("magnet gists") — the original 4 found by F-1370 (e.g. "The Sky Is Falling"), then dozens more once J-1001/F-1384 extended the same tag-based dry-run past D-0221's original 25-title/128-gist sample to the full remaining ~103 titles (final combined picture: 128 flagged gists, 115 titles, 12,005 fragments already correctly filed against 14,707 misfiled). Clearing a fragment's `chapter_id` (→ NULL) is the module's own documented reversible undo — it never deletes; the fragment simply re-enters ordinary recall until `sweep()`'s next pass re-files it under the fixed logic.

D-0221 (opened 2026-09-14 by J-0964/F-1360) asks whether the corpus-wide repair may proceed as an autonomous job-queue-dispatched batch, given its scale (10,000+ UPDATE statements at full scope) and the founder's own standing priority on memory work. It has been ruled three times and reopened three times as new evidence changed the picture each time: ruling 1 asked for a hand-reviewed sample first (met by F-1370/J-0993); ruling 2 authorized capped, skip-untagged batches but was reopened when J-1001/F-1384's fuller sweep found a materially larger scope and many more magnet gists than the original ruling anticipated; ruling 3 ("proceed, extra care on ALL magnets") was itself reopened within hours, 2026-09-15, on a narrow sub-question J-1015 surfaced while trying to actually execute the one already-prepared batch: does a batch whose specific target `fragment_id`s were already individually hand-reviewed (not a bulk clear of a magnet gist's full contents) still need its own separate review pass, given it happens to touch a gist that is independently a confirmed magnet? See `decide.py show D-0221` for the full ruling/defeater/reopen text, not reproduced here. Zero live writes have occurred anywhere in this investigation to date — every session in the chain has independently reconfirmed `data/memory/nascence.db`'s own `LastWriteTime` unchanged since 2026-09-13 10:24:19, most recently J-1015/F-1414.
